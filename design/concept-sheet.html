OVERTONE

Fly through a cosmos that plays music. Every enemy keeps time, every shot is a note, and a living orb of sound rides on your nose. Hold the beam, wait for the beat, and let it ring.

The landscape, far to near

Movement I is drawn in code as pixel art, in seven layers that scroll at their own speeds. Every layer answers the music somewhere. Use Landscape only above to see it without the action. Movements II and III use the same layers with their own shapes and palettes.

Your craft & its Echo

Pick a craft drawn from above with the nose up. The game turns it to fly right (the large picture shows it that way) and squashes it a quarter as it banks into a climb or dive, so every pixel stays square.

The ensemble

Eight roles from a neon cosmos: creatures and machines, not instruments, but every one of them keeps time. Each one either warns you before it fires (a white flash for half a beat) or shows a chevron at the edge a beat before it arrives.

Pickups & bosses

Couriers drop pickups. Each pickup carries a glyph drawn over it, so you can tell them apart without colour.

How a movement plays

Three movementsI Pulse (128 BPM, a dusk over the instrument city), II Strings (140 BPM, a cave strung with vibrating strings), III Crescendo (152 BPM, everything at once). A new wave arrives every bar. About 2½ minutes each, then a boss. After III the ensemble plays again, faster.
Everything keeps timeEnemies move and fire on the beat, and anything about to fire turns white for the half-beat before. The beat lamp shows where you are in the bar.
Fire, or chargeHold FIRE for a steady stream. Hold CHARGE instead (you stop shooting while you do) and a ring round your craft fills one segment per beat, up to three. Release for a beam that pierces everything.
ResonateRelease with two or more segments exactly on the beat (the ring goes solid white) for a RESONANT beam: half as wide again, double points, and every kill adds 1 to HARMONY.
Harmony ×1–×8Multiplies every point. Resonant kills and CHORD bonuses (a whole formation) raise it. It drops back to ×1 when you're hit.
The EchoCan't be destroyed. Touch it to attach it to your nose or tail. Attached, it blocks shots and fires the crystal's weapon (backwards from the tail). Launched, it hovers ahead and repeats your shots. Recall brings it home.
Hits cost powerA hit takes a Harmonic first, then a weapon level, then knocks the Echo loose (it drifts until you touch it). Hit with nothing left and you lose a ship, respawn on the spot, and blink for 2 s. The frame is a hit too, and bounces you off.
3 ships+1 every 50,000. At game over you can continue from the start of the movement with your score reset, or start again from Movement I. HI keeps your best full run.

From the 1987 original

Overtone borrows the mechanics of Irem's R-Type. The name, setting, characters, levels and art are new.

Kept
  • A side-scroller with terrain to thread
  • Hold to charge a beam that pierces
  • An indestructible pod for nose or tail that blocks shots, launches and comes back
  • Bits above and below, speed-ups and homing missiles
  • Three weapons from crystals, levelled by repeats
  • Carriers that drop power-ups, a boss per stage
Changed
  • A neon cosmos that keeps time instead of a biomechanical empire
  • Separate fire and charge buttons: no mashing
  • Hits cost power, not lives; terrain bounces you off
  • Respawn on the spot, no checkpoint restarts
  • Crystals cycle their weapon so you choose
Added
  • Everything on the beat, with a half-beat warning
  • Resonant releases and the Harmony multiplier
  • Shots pitched by height: fly higher, play higher
  • A landscape that plays along: the frame, the moon, the skyline
  • Controller and touch from day one
